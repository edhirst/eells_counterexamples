# A counterexample to Eells' conjecture: numerics and computer-assisted certificate

This repository contains the code behind the paper *A counterexample to Eells' conjecture via a
computer-assisted proof* (E. Hirst, D. Platt, H. N. Sá Earp, J. Stein): the construction of the
approximate harmonic maps $\bar W_q:S^3\to\mathbb R^3$ and the rigorous verification of every
constant in Table A.1 of the paper, i.e. the Newton–Kantorovich certificate that a genuine
equivariant harmonic map $S^3\to S^2$ of Hopf invariant $q$ exists near $\bar W_q/|\bar W_q|$,
for $q=2$ (the main theorem) and $q=3,4,5,6$ (Remark rem:family), together with the energy of
these maps and, for $q=2$, the lower bound $9$ for the Morse index.

The construction for Hopf invariant $q$ uses the group $G_q$ generated by
$e^{i\theta}\cdot(z,w)=(e^{i\theta}z,e^{-iq\theta}w)$ (acting on the target by $\zeta\mapsto e^{-2iq\theta}\zeta$),
complex conjugation $\kappa$ and $S(z,w)=(z,-w)$ (acting by $t\mapsto-t$); $G_2$ is the group $G$
of Section 2.1.  The equivariant basis of Proposition 2.2 becomes
$(\mathrm{Re}\,Y_{qn-2q,n,j},\mathrm{Im}\,Y_{qn-2q,n,j},0)$, $n$ even, and $\sqrt2(0,0,\mathrm{Re}\,Y_{qn,n,j})$,
$n$ odd, and the reference map is $V_q=(w^2-\alpha_q^{-2}\bar z^{2q},\,2\alpha_q^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(z^qw))$.
Everything else (the equation $F(v)=0$, the estimates of Sections 3 and 4) is as in the paper.
The code follows the notation of the paper; each module says which statement it implements.

| 2 | 40 | 40 | 1.987e-8 | 23.67 | 9.5e-7 | [1.58661993295, 1.58661993297] | 1 min |
| 3 | 80 | 40 | 5.18e-11 | 48.63 | 5.1e-9 | [1.97074310310, 1.97074310315] | 2 min |
| 4 | 112 | 60 | 3.855e-11 | 77.67 | 6e-9 | [2.25147044354, 2.25147044362] | 5 min |
| 5 | 152 | 60 | 4.657e-11 | 122.7 | 1.2e-8 | [2.47161001814, 2.47161001826] | 10 min |
| 6 | 184 | 80 | 6.482e-11 | 165.6 | 2.2e-8 | [2.65238168307, 2.65238168322] | 15 min |

All five certificates pass (`certificate/output/q<q>_D<D>_l<l>.json`, `.log`).  The certificate is
deterministic: re-running it on the same data reproduces every bound bit for bit (no random
numbers are used, and the outputs do not depend on the number of BLAS threads).  For $q=2$ the
index step certifies nine negative directions: $\lambda^*\le-0.04109$, $\delta\le2.798\cdot10^{-4}$.

## Layout

```
eells/            library (runs on plain floats for the numerics and on rigorous balls for the certificate)
  ball.py           midpoint–radius interval arithmetic on numpy arrays (the only source of rigour)
  grid.py           orbit-space grid (|z|^2, phi): exact Clenshaw–Curtis × trapezoid quadrature,
                    Chebyshev–Fourier coefficients and rigorous sup/inf bounds of G_q-invariant polynomials
  harmonics.py      Jacobi basis Y_{m,n,j}, Lemma 2.1, the G_q-equivariant fields of Proposition 2.2,
                    values / tangential gradients / Laplacians on the grid
  fields.py         Wbar = sum_k a_k Psi_k from a coefficient file
  equation.py       the map F (eq. F-equation), its linearisation (eq. linearisation), the expanded
                    coefficients P, M_a, M_0 of Section 3.3 and the L^2-adjoint L^* of the linearisation
  reference.py      the reference field V_q, the Hopf map, the exact Sobolev constants c_inf, c_4, c_5, beta
                    of Propositions 2.3 and 2.4
  sectors.py        Section 5 (q = 2): C^3-valued modes P, M, T built from Y_{m,n,j}, the sectors S_{nu,p}
                    of the circle character nu and the antipodal parity p, real fields from complex sectors
numerics/         non-rigorous floating point
  solve_approximate.py   Newton–Galerkin construction of Wbar_q (Section 3.1)
  index_basis.py         Section 5: negative eigenvectors of the Galerkin matrix of Q_Wbar in each sector,
                         rounded to Gaussian-integer test fields
certificate/      rigorous verification of Table A.1
  approximate_bounds.py  Proposition 3.1: residual, energy, homotopy constant
  linear_estimate.py     Section 3.3: eta, b_1, b_0, gamma, A, ||B||, ||C||, ||A^{-1} B||, ||L^{-1}||, M
  nonlinear_estimate.py  Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 2.10: C_nl, the radius r and the final inequalities
  energy_estimate.py     Proposition 4.2: the energy of the harmonic map
  index_estimate.py      Section 5 (q = 2): delta, the matrices G, H of the nine test fields, lambda^* and the
                         check that G + delta H is negative definite (Morse index >= 9)
  certify.py             driver: runs all steps, writes certificate/output/q<q>_D<D>_l<l>.json
  certify_index.py       stand-alone run of the index step from an existing certificate
  make_tables.py         the appendix tables (Table A.1 for all q, leading coefficients, index test fields)
data/             coefficient files Wbar_q<q>_D<D>.json (exact IEEE doubles), the continuation starts,
                  solver logs, index_basis_q2.json (the test fields of Section 5)
tests/            unit tests (basis orthonormality for every q, Green's identity, operator and adjoint
                  identities, enclosures, ...)
environment/      requirements.txt and setup instructions
```

See [environment/README.md](environment/README.md). In short:


## Running the code

All commands are run from the repository root.

1. **Approximate solutions** (floating point, seconds to a few minutes).  Finds $\bar W_q$ of
   degree $D$ in the span of the basis fields of Proposition 2.2 by Newton's method on the
   Galerkin system $\langle\Psi_k,\,F(0)+\kappa(|\bar W|^2-1)\bar W\rangle=0$.  The first run starts
   from the projection of $V_q/|V_q|$ (for $q\ge3$ at a low degree, where this converges); the
   degree is then raised by continuation (`--start`):

   ```bash
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 2 --degree 40
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 3 --degree 32 --out data/start/Wbar_q3_D32.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 3 --degree 80 --start data/start/Wbar_q3_D32.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 4 --degree 40 --out data/start/Wbar_q4_D40.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 4 --degree 72 --start data/start/Wbar_q4_D40.json --out data/start/Wbar_q4_D72.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 4 --degree 112 --start data/start/Wbar_q4_D72.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 5 --degree 40 --out data/start/Wbar_q5_D40.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 5 --degree 96 --start data/start/Wbar_q5_D40.json --out data/start/Wbar_q5_D96.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 5 --degree 152 --start data/start/Wbar_q5_D96.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 6 --degree 48 --out data/start/Wbar_q6_D48.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 6 --degree 112 --start data/start/Wbar_q6_D48.json --out data/start/Wbar_q6_D112.json
   python -m numerics.solve_approximate --hopf 6 --degree 184 --start data/start/Wbar_q6_D112.json
   ```

2. **Certificate** (rigorous; from under a minute for $q=2$ to about an hour for $q=6$, most of it
   the one-off interval Jacobi tables, which are cached in `certificate/output/cache`).  Verifies
   every row of Table A.1 for the given $\bar W_q$ and cutoff $l$, checks the Newton–Kantorovich
   inequalities $Mk<1$, $M(\varepsilon+kr)\le r$ and $C_{\text{homotopy}}-c_\infty r>0$, encloses the
   energy of the harmonic map (Proposition 4.2, with the check $\|w\|_{C^0}<1$) and, for $q=2$,
   runs the index step:

   ```bash
   python -u -m certificate.certify --coefficients data/Wbar_q2_D40.json  --cutoff 40
   python -u -m certificate.certify --coefficients data/Wbar_q3_D80.json  --cutoff 40
   python -u -m certificate.certify --coefficients data/Wbar_q4_D112.json --cutoff 60
   python -u -m certificate.certify --coefficients data/Wbar_q5_D152.json --cutoff 60
   python -u -m certificate.certify --coefficients data/Wbar_q6_D184.json --cutoff 80
   ```

   Output: `certificate/output/q<q>_D<D>_l<l>.json` (all bounds and intermediate quantities).

3. **Morse index, lower bound** ($q=2$, Section 5).  The second variation at $u=W/|W|$ is studied
   through the form $Q_W$ of eq. (quadraticform) on all $\mathbb R^3$-valued fields.  The
   floating-point step diagonalises the Galerkin matrix of $Q_{\bar W}$ in each sector $S_{\nu,p}$
   (circle character $\nu$, antipodal parity $p$; `eells/sectors.py`) on the modes of degree $\le8$,
   and rounds the eigenvectors with negative eigenvalue to Gaussian-integer combinations (largest
   coefficient $100$): five complex fields $f_1,\dots,f_5$ in the sectors $(0,-)$, $(1,-)$, $(2,-)$,
   $(2,+)$, $(3,-)$, i.e. nine real directions:

   ```bash
   python -m numerics.index_basis --coefficients data/Wbar_q2_D40.json --max-degree 8
   ```

   `certificate.certify` then computes $\delta$ (Proposition prop:quadraticestimate), the matrices
   $G_{ab}=Q_{\bar W}(\xi_a,\xi_b)$ and $H_{ab}=\langle\xi_a,\xi_b\rangle_{H^1}$ of the nine fields in ball
   arithmetic, $\lambda^*$, and verifies that $G+\delta H$ is negative definite, so that the Morse
   index of the harmonic map is at least $9$.  The step can also be run alone:

   ```bash
   python -m certificate.certify_index --certificate certificate/output/q2_D40_l40.json
   ```

   This certifies only the lower bound; the upper bound (index $\le 9$) is not part of this repository.

4. **Appendix tables** (Table A.1 for all $q$, the leading coefficients of $\bar W_q$, the index test fields):

   ```bash
   python -m certificate.make_tables certificate/output/q2_D40_l40.json certificate/output/q3_D80_l40.json \
       certificate/output/q4_D112_l60.json certificate/output/q5_D152_l60.json certificate/output/q6_D184_l80.json
   ```

## How the rigour works

* Every quantity of Table A.1 is computed in the ball arithmetic of `eells/ball.py`, which
  encloses rounding errors of IEEE double arithmetic by the standard a-priori bounds (no
  directed rounding, so any platform/BLAS is fine).  The few transcendental constants ($\pi$,
  $\cos$, $\sin$ at rational multiples of $\pi$, the Jacobi-polynomial tables) are enclosed with
  mpmath interval arithmetic at high precision and rounded outward.
* Integrals of $G$-invariant polynomials over $S^3$ are reduced to the orbit space
  $(|z|^2,\varphi)$ and computed by a Clenshaw–Curtis × trapezoidal rule that is **exact** for the
  polynomial degree at hand (the degree of every integrand is tracked in `eells/equation.py`), so
  the only error is rounding, which the balls enclose.
* Suprema and infima (the constants $\eta,b_1,b_0$, $\|\bar W\|_{C^0}$, $C_{\text{homotopy}}$,
  $\min|\bar W|$, the energy error) come from the exact Chebyshev–Fourier coefficients of the polynomial in
  question: the expansion is evaluated on a fine grid and a Lipschitz remainder is added.
* The coefficients of $\bar W_q$ are IEEE doubles and are used exactly (the files in `data/` are
  the polynomials that were certified); the Sobolev constants $c_\infty,c_4,c_5,\beta$ enter as
  balls enclosing their exact values.
* The norms of the coupling blocks $B$ and $C$ of Section 3.3 are obtained from Gram matrices of the
  low modes, $C^TC=G-A^TA$ and $BB^T=G^*-AA^T$ with $G_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\mathscr L\Psi_\alpha,\mathscr L\Psi_\beta\rangle$,
  $G^*_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\mathscr L^*\Psi_\alpha,\mathscr L^*\Psi_\beta\rangle$ and the $L^2$-adjoint $\mathscr L^*$
  (`eells/equation.py`), all integrated exactly; this avoids computing the $O(l^2(l+4\deg\bar W)^2)$
  individual entries.
* In the index step the test fields are polynomials too, but $Q_{\bar W}$ has the rational
  coefficients $|\bar W|^{-2}$, $|\bar W|^{-4}$.  The certificate integrates the polynomial proxy
  (coefficients replaced by $1$) exactly and bounds the difference by Cauchy–Schwarz with
  $\sup|\,|\bar W|^2-1|$ (about $10^{-9}$); see `certificate/index_estimate.py`.  Only fields of the same
  sector are integrated against each other (their products are $G$-invariant polynomials, so the
  orbit-space quadrature applies); different sectors are orthogonal by the $G$-invariance of
  $Q_{\bar W}$ and of the $H^1$ product, and for $\nu\ne0$ the two real fields $\Re f,\Im f$ of a
  complex field $f$ give the block $\tfrac12 Q^h(f,f)\,I_2$ (`eells/sectors.py`).
